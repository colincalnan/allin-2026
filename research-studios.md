# ALL IN 2026: studio and people research

Conference: ALL IN 2026, Palais des congrès de Montréal, September 16-17 2026. Canada's largest AI event, 200+ speakers across four stages and five content tracks (AI & Society, Applied AI & Enterprise Transformation, AI at the Frontier, AI Data & Infrastructure, Physical AI & Robotics). Source: [allinevent.ai](https://allinevent.ai/pages/2026-programming), [allinevent.ai/pages/2026-speakers](https://allinevent.ai/pages/2026-speakers).

Two things worth knowing before you plan the two days.

There is a **Media & Entertainment** vertical, one of eleven, covering how AI is transforming media, entertainment and the creative industries, from content creation and production to audience analysis, personalisation, distribution and discovery. Its dedicated programming is an Agora stage session titled **Creative Industries**, featuring David Lerech (Propulsion Media) alongside an unnamed Runway executive. The Runway person's name was not published at time of research; treat that as unverified. Media organisations attending include La Presse, Radio-Canada, The Globe and Mail, Financial Post and TIME. Source: [allinevent.ai/pages/2026-industries](https://allinevent.ai/pages/2026-industries), [allinevent.ai/pages/2026-programming](https://allinevent.ai/pages/2026-programming). The detailed schedule was not published on the programming page when checked.

The **Startup Zone** includes Gallea, **Sandscape** and Konpanion, described as building AI-assisted creation tools. Source: [allinevent.ai/pages/2026-industries](https://allinevent.ai/pages/2026-industries). That is where to find Laurent Bernier rather than hunting the floor.

**Do not rely on bumping into anyone.** The conference app, live since August 11, lets you browse the programme, build a personal agenda and **schedule meetings**. Source: [allinevent.ai/pages/2026-edition](https://allinevent.ai/pages/2026-edition). The top three or four names below should be meeting requests sent before you fly, not floor-walking hope. The floor also has a VIP Lounge (Cohere), four AI Tours and three AI Challenges, across four stages.

**The one thing on this page with a deadline.** SAT's **Research-Creation Residency closes 18 October 2026**. $10,000, two months, access to SAT labs and teams, and explicitly open to "artistes professionnels et collectifs artistiques du monde entier," international artists included. One of its three research areas is **"AI & generative AI: interactive, real-time generative audiovisual technology using AI,"** built on **Koaia**, SAT's own Stable Diffusion plus TensorRT real-time visual tool. Free online info session **30 September 2026, 11:00 Montreal time**. Contact residences@sat.qc.ca. Everything else in this document is a conversation. This is the only item with a date on it, and it is the closest thing found anywhere to a funded, open, remote-eligible route into exactly the work Colin says he wants.

**None of these people are speaking.** Not one of the named attendees appears on the ALL IN 2026 programming page ([allinevent.ai/pages/2026-programming](https://allinevent.ai/pages/2026-programming)). These are floor and networking conversations at a 6,500 to 7,500 person event ([techbeat.ca](https://techbeat.ca/canada-tech-events/all-in-2026-montreal-canadas-largest-ai-conference/)), which is another reason to book meetings in the app rather than hope.

**Companion file.** The broadcast and media-company attendees (Québecor, Zoomer Media, MediaTonik, Angle Media Group, Radio-Canada, Bridge Média) are covered in `research-media.md` in this folder. That file also explains **E-AI (Entertain-AI)**, the Montreal entertainment-and-AI body whose 2026 summit ran 18-19 February 2026 and whose next edition is 25-26 February 2027 at the Palais des congrès.

**One correction to carry across from that file.** It describes E-AI as founded by roughly 50 organisations including Moment Factory, Behaviour Interactive, Centre PHI and the SAT, citing E-AI's own 2021 founding announcement. Two independent checks of E-AI's **current** partner, speaker and about pages found **Moment Factory (partner, and Céline Mornet holds a board seat) and the SAT (partner)** confirmed, but **Behaviour Interactive and Centre PHI appear nowhere**. Rodeo FX and PixMob appear as speakers only. Treat the 2021 founding list as historical and the current membership as the shorter one.

A note on method. Everything below carries a URL. Where a company has almost no findable web presence, that is stated as the finding rather than filled in with plausible guesses. Several of these are one-person or two-person operations and the research reflects that honestly.

## 1. Sarah Mackenzie, Director, MUTEK Forum and Creative Development, MUTEK

The most relevant person on the entire list to what Colin actually says he wants.

**Who she is.** Cultural strategist, documentary filmmaker and media arts curator based in Montréal, working at the intersection of art, technology and social impact. She is Director of MUTEK Forum and Creative Development, and in that role she leads both the annual symposium and the AI Ecologies Lab. Past work for Red Bull Music Academy, CHANEL, Coach, the Venice Biennale of Architecture and CBC. She has presented at SXSW, AVA London, Art Basel and C2 Montreal, and is co-directing a feature documentary with EyeSteelFilm about identity in the digital age. Source: [forum.mutek.org/en/speakers/sarah-mackenzie](https://forum.mutek.org/en/speakers/sarah-mackenzie). Personal site: [smackenzie.ca](https://smackenzie.ca/About).

**What MUTEK is.** A Montreal non-profit festival organisation for electronic music and digital art. The team page lists roughly 60 people across leadership, production, curation, communications, the Village Numérique, and Forum/Market coordination. Notably the technical layer is contracted rather than staffed: technical direction is Productions Main Droite, sound is HQ Audio, lighting is CR34TE, backline is Le Dep du Tech, and there is an Event Technology Consultant (Christopher Lemmonier). Source: [montreal.mutek.org/en/team](https://montreal.mutek.org/en/team).

That contracting model is the actionable detail. MUTEK does not appear to employ real-time visual engineers in-house. It buys them in per production. That is a route in for a freelancer or contractor, and it is a different route from applying for a job.

**What MUTEK Forum is.** The professional and industry strand, correct. MUTEK Forum 2026 ran August 26-28 2026 at Atrium et Studio-Théâtre des Grands Ballets and Édifice Wilder, under the theme "Symbiotic Frequencies," exploring dissolving boundaries between bodies, technologies and ecosystems. Format is lectures, keynotes, panels, workshops and artist-led masterclasses. Attendees are described as artists, institutions, technology professionals and digital experts across music, AI, XR, ecology, media art, gaming and design. Source: [forum.mutek.org](https://forum.mutek.org/en/news/mutek-forum-2026-announces-expanded-programming-under-theme-symbiotic-frequencies).

**MUTEK Market and MUTEK Match.** The Forum includes MUTEK Market, with "MUTEK Match," a 1:1 matchmaking programme connecting participants with international presenters and curators. Past attending institutions include the Barbican, the V&A and the Tate. Source: same page as above. This is the closest thing MUTEK has to a formal business-development channel, and it is curator-facing rather than technologist-facing.

**Route in for a technologist rather than an artist.** Three findings, in order of usefulness.

The AI Ecologies Lab is an interdisciplinary residency at the intersection of AI, art and ecology, explicitly bringing together "artists, researchers, and technologists." Six participants are selected. It runs in phases: a virtual lab with seminars and 1:1 coaching, then a week-long in-person lab in Montréal including workshops at Concordia and a hackathon-style sprint at the SAT to build technical prototypes, with a presentation slot at MUTEK Forum at the end. Partners are the SAT, the Milieux Institute (Concordia), the Abundant Intelligences Program and the Applied AI Institute. Source: [forum.mutek.org/en/mutek-ai-ecologies-lab](https://forum.mutek.org/en/mutek-ai-ecologies-lab), [forum.mutek.org/en/news/mutek-ai-ecologies-lab-participants](https://forum.mutek.org/en/news/mutek-ai-ecologies-lab-participants).

The documented cycle ran call January 13 to February 14, with notification March 3, virtual lab April, in-person lab June 2-6, presentation at Forum in August. The page does not explicitly restrict applicants to artists, and the framing of "bridging artistic, curatorial and technical disciplines" suggests technologists are eligible, but it does not say so definitively. That is the single most useful question to ask Sarah Mackenzie in person: can a technologist apply to the AI Ecologies Lab without an artist collaborator, and when does the next call open.

The festival Call for Projects is the artist route and is a poorer fit. It is for live musical performances (unreleased, primarily electronic), digital audiovisual performances exploring the image-sound relationship, and projects using emerging technologies. Open to Canadian citizens, Indigenous artists from these territories, and foreign artists resident in Québec or Canada, so Colin is eligible on nationality. Deadline for the 27th edition (August 25-30 2026) was January 31 2026. DJ mixes are not accepted. The page says nothing about non-performing collaborators or technologist roles; the contact for clarification is soumission@mutek.org. Source: [montreal.mutek.org/en/news/call-for-projects-2026](https://montreal.mutek.org/en/news/call-for-projects-2026).

Historical precedent that technologists are welcome: the earlier MUTEK AI Art Lab (Montreal edition, March 2020) selected 14 artists from six countries out of 70 applicants, and its programme leadership included an engineer from Stradigi AI (Habib Hajimolahoseini) alongside curators. Source: [montreal.mutek.org/en/news/mutek-ai-art-lab](https://montreal.mutek.org/en/news/mutek-ai-art-lab).

**Verdict: seek her out first.** She is the only person on either list whose day job is the exact intersection Colin has named and has not yet entered. The specific ask is not "do you have work," it is "what does the path look like for someone who builds the pipelines rather than performs." She commissions, curates and convenes, which means she knows every studio and every freelance real-time person in Montreal. One good conversation here is worth more than five vendor conversations.

## 2. Jean-Michaël Celerier, Director of Technology Development, Société des arts technologiques (SAT)

Read this one twice. On the evidence found, this is the single best-matched person at the conference for Colin's actual technical profile, and the coordinator's hunch about ossia score was right.

**The ossia connection is confirmed, not assumed.** Jean-Michaël Celerier is the lead developer and software architect of **ossia score**, an interactive sequencer for the intermedia arts that combines non-linear timelines with the dataflow paradigm so artists can build evolving multimedia works, musical pieces and museum installations. He is a co-founder of ossia. Sources: [ossia.io/about.html](https://ossia.io/about.html), [ossia.io/project.html](https://ossia.io/project.html), [jcelerier.name](https://jcelerier.name/).

**And he is the SAT technology director.** Listed as Director of Technology Development at the Société des arts technologiques, based in Montreal, leading SAT's technology department. Sources: [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/jcelerier/), [RocketReach](https://rocketreach.co/jean-michael-celerier-email_252820507). Note his title reads "Director of Technology Development" rather than CTO in these sources; the attendee-list title of CTO is close but treat the exact wording as unverified.

**Background.** Born in France in 1992. Engineering studies and a doctorate at ENSEIRB-MATMECA and Université de Bordeaux in computer science and multimedia, with a 2018 thesis on authoring temporal media, following the ANR OSSIA research project (2012-2015). Describes himself as a freelance researcher interested in art, code, computer music and interactive show control. Past employers listed: Blue Yeti, KDAB, SCRIME, Rock & Chansons, and Université Concordia. Sources: [jcelerier.name](https://jcelerier.name/), [ossia.io/project.html](https://ossia.io/project.html), [RocketReach](https://rocketreach.co/jean-michael-celerier-email_252820507).

**Public open-source work, substantial and verifiable.** GitHub handle [jcelerier](https://github.com/jcelerier), based in Montréal, 410 followers, primary language C++, member of 13+ organisations including ossia, jamoma, NVIDIA GameWorks, linuxaudio and scrime-u-bordeaux. Pinned repositories:

- **ossia/score**, interactive sequencer for the intermedia arts, 2.1k stars
- **celtera/libremidi**, modern C++ MIDI 1 and MIDI 2 real-time and file I/O library, 705 stars
- **celtera/avendish**, "declarative polyamorous cross-system intermedia objects," 488 stars
- **qml-creative-controls**, QML controls for creative applications, 278 stars
- **ossia/libossia**, modern C++ distributed object model for creative coding, 231 stars
- **cpp-teaching-manifesto**

Source: [github.com/jcelerier](https://github.com/jcelerier). His stated toolchain is C++, Qt, Boost and CMake, developing on Linux, favouring AGPLv3, and working with openFrameworks, Faust and PureData ([jcelerier.name](https://jcelerier.name/)). ossia's supporting organisations have included LaBRI, SCRIME, Mozilla, Unreal Engine, OpenCollective and the French Ministry of Culture ([ossia.io/about.html](https://ossia.io/about.html)).

**What ossia score actually is, technically.** A multimedia sequencer combining temporal design with data and audio processing, letting you build interactive scores that synchronise music, video and other media with precise timing. Documented support: **OSC and OSCQuery, MIDI (MIDI 2.0 planned), Art-Net, Syphon and Spout, VST3, LV2, JSFX, Faust compiler integration, a WebSockets remote-control API, and Interactive Shader Format**. It reads H.264 and HAP video, applies shader-based filters, and renders through Vulkan, Metal, D3D or OpenGL depending on platform. Development traces to the i-score research project at LaBRI in the late 1990s; Celerier led the v2+ rewrite during his doctorate. Funded by community donations through OpenCollective. Source: [ossia.io/project.html](https://ossia.io/project.html). TouchDesigner, Unreal, Unity, NDI and DMX integration were not addressed in that source (unverified).

Art-Net, Syphon and Spout, and ISF shaders are the giveaway. This is a genuine show-control and real-time media tool, not an academic toy.

**The detail that turns this from interesting into actionable.** He teaches a course, "Développement logiciel pour la création numérique," run jointly by SAT and ÉTS Formation, described as teaching developers to write extensions for digital art software. It covers media art environments and C/C++ fundamentals, then **plug-in creation for Max/MSP and TouchDesigner**, debugging, and advanced audio signal processing, video processing, graphical interfaces and multi-threaded communication. Named platforms: **Max/MSP, TouchDesigner, Blender and ossia score**. Languages: C++, C, Python. The documented run was 8 full days (April 16-23 2025), in person at ÉTS Formation in Montreal, $1,037 CAD, with a prerequisite of basic C, C++ or Python competency. Source: [sat.qc.ca formation page](https://sat.qc.ca/fr/formations/developpement-logiciel-pour-la-creation-numerique/).

That is a paid, structured, Montreal-based bridge from "senior developer who writes pipelines" to "person who extends TouchDesigner in C++," taught by the man running technology at the institution that owns the Satosphère. It is the most concrete route into the real-time world found anywhere in this research, and Colin already has the prerequisite.

**He teaches this stuff publicly, repeatedly.** Beyond the ÉTS course, he ran a hands-on ossia score workshop at Hexagram / Speculative Life Concordia (EV Building, 1515 Sainte-Catherine W) in March 2023, aimed explicitly at "media artists experienced with software like Max/MSP, PureData, Unity3D, or TouchDesigner who want to explore free, open-source alternatives for time-based creative work," and gave a demo the day before at the **Art & Code meetup at SAT**. He is described there as the software's principal developer and a Concordia postdoc. Sources: [hexagram.ca ossia workshop](https://hexagram.ca/en/atelier-ossia-score/), [Milieux event listing](https://milieux.concordia.ca/event/workshop-ossia-by-jean-michael-celerier/).

"Art & Code meetup at SAT" is worth chasing separately as a recurring Montreal community night.

**What to ask him.** Whether the course runs again and whether any part is remote. Whether SAT contracts external software developers on projects. What ossia is missing that a pipeline engineer could contribute. That last one is the strongest opening, because it is a contributor conversation, not a job conversation, and he has 2.1k stars' worth of reasons to answer it.

**Verdict: find him first, jointly with Sarah Mackenzie.** Mackenzie is the door to the scene; Celerier is the door to the code. He is the only person on any of these lists whose public work Colin could read on the plane and comment on intelligently.

### The institution itself

Founded 1996, non-profit, described as having a triple mission as a centre for artists, research and training, with a leading role in immersive technologies, augmented reality and creative use of high-speed networks. Source: [mtl.org listing](https://www.mtl.org/en/what-to-do/culture-arts-heritage/sat-society-for-arts-and-technology), [sat.qc.ca](https://sat.qc.ca/en/satosphere/).

**The Satosphère**, opened October 2011, is described as the first permanent immersive modular theatre: 18 metres in diameter, 11.5 to 13 metres high, a 180 or 210 degree vertical by 360 degree screen driven by **8 video projectors**, with **93 full-range speakers plus 5 low-frequency channels**, capacity 350. More than **180 immersive works** (films, performances, installations, concerts) have come out of its artist residency programme. Source: [sat.qc.ca/en/satosphere](https://sat.qc.ca/en/satosphere/).

The page does not publish software specifications, delivery formats, resolution, residency application process or calls for content. The stated contact for all of that is the IMMERSION department, info@sat.qc.ca, 514-844-2033 #212. Asking for the technical delivery spec is a low-cost, high-signal first email: it is a normal question from a technologist and it gets you a named person in the department.

**The Research-Creation Residency, and Koaia.** This is the single most actionable item in the whole document. The residency **closes 18 October 2026**: $10,000, two months, access to SAT labs and teams, explicitly open to "artistes professionnels et collectifs artistiques du monde entier," international artists included, so BC is no barrier. One of its three research areas is **"AI & generative AI: interactive, real-time generative audiovisual technology using AI,"** built around **Koaia**, SAT's own real-time visual tool running **Stable Diffusion with TensorRT**. There is a free online information session on **30 September 2026 at 11:00 Montreal time**. Contact residences@sat.qc.ca.

Koaia is the detail worth sitting with. A publicly funded Montreal institution has built a real-time Stable Diffusion tool for dome projection and is paying outsiders to come and push it. That is Colin's stack, Colin's interest, and an open door with a date on it.

**They build software.** SAT's R&D page names their own tools: LivePose (an online tool that recognises silhouettes from video streams, using machine learning for pose detection and action recognition across multiple cameras), Satellite XR (a web platform built on Mozilla Hubs for immersive virtual and hybrid experiences), and Scenic (a telepresence solution connecting 23 remote rooms). They also advise on standard open-source processing and streaming stacks, naming GStreamer and FFmpeg. Source: [sat.qc.ca/en/r-and-d](https://sat.qc.ca/en/r-and-d/).

LivePose is directly adjacent to Colin's interest. Camera-in, pose-out, driving visuals, is exactly the interactive-performance primitive.

They run an alliance called **ECLO**, co-founded with **Zú** (an earlier machine-read of this page rendered the co-founder as "Zentopia," which does not exist; see section 27), with academic partners Concordia, UQAM and NAD-UQAC, service partners Les 7 Doigts, NRF and PINQ2, and corporate partners Moment Factory and Banque Nationale. Source: same R&D page, cross-checked against [zumtl.com/en](https://zumtl.com/en/).

**Hiring.** At time of research the careers page listed only two roles: Communications Project Manager (B2B Strategy) and IT Support Technician. No developer or creative technologist roles were open. They do offer a hybrid remote model. Applications go to carrieres@sat.qc.ca. Source: [sat.qc.ca/en/careers](https://sat.qc.ca/en/careers/).

But their hiring history matters more than the current snapshot: SAT recurrently creates multimedia, full-stack and **TouchDesigner trainer** roles. They are a periodic employer of exactly the profile Colin is building toward, currently between openings.

**Verdict: a place, not a person, but keep it on the radar.** No open technical role right now. The realistic engagement is the AI Ecologies Lab hackathon sprint (which is hosted at the SAT) or watching that careers page.

## 3. Above The Void, Montreal

This is the answer to the "why did Runway show up in this profile" question, and it is the second most interesting entry in the original list.

**What they are.** A Montreal generative cinema studio, founded in Montréal in 2022 by artist-technologists Luc Leclerc and Laura Weselak. They make music videos, experimental films and interactive installations using AI-driven visual storytelling. Sources: [abovethevoid.com/about-us.php](https://www.abovethevoid.com/about-us.php?lang=en), [ALL IN exhibitor listing](https://app.swapcard.com/widget/event/all-in-2026/exhibitor/RXhoaWJpdG9yXzI1MDQ2MjM=).

Note a small discrepancy worth resolving in person: the ALL IN exhibitor listing and secondary coverage describe the studio as "founded by filmmaker and technologist Luc Leclerc" alone, while the studio's own about page names two founders, Leclerc and Weselak. Trust the about page.

**Why "Runway" is in the profile.** Above The Void is part of Runway ML's Creative Partner Program and has been hosting AI meetups with Runway ML in Montreal. Source: [abovethevoid.com/about-us.php](https://www.abovethevoid.com/about-us.php?lang=en). They are also a creative partner of Dreamina.

That is the single most useful fact in this document after the MUTEK entry. It means there is a recurring, local, in-person AI film-making meetup in Montreal with a direct Runway relationship, and its organisers are exhibiting at the conference.

**Tools, named explicitly.** Deforum, WarpFusion, Automatic1111 and ComfyUI. Source: [abovethevoid.com/about-us.php](https://www.abovethevoid.com/about-us.php?lang=en). This is a real technical stack, not marketing language. Anyone naming WarpFusion and Automatic1111 has been doing this since the diffusion-video era rather than arriving with the Sora wave.

**DreamReel** is their proprietary AI cinematic production platform, built around a structured Act/Scene/Beat/Shot hierarchy with multi-agent AI orchestration, described as the internal instrument through which they produce work and collaborate with partner organisations. Source: [ALL IN exhibitor listing](https://app.swapcard.com/widget/event/all-in-2026/exhibitor/RXhoaWJpdG9yXzI1MDQ2MjM=).

DreamReel is structurally the same shape as what Colin builds: a pipeline that takes structured input and orchestrates model calls into finished media. That is a concrete thing to talk about rather than a generic networking conversation.

**Recognition.** Venice Biennale 2024 finalist for "William, or The Fragile Harmony of Latent Spaces," plus film festival selections and awards. Source: [ALL IN exhibitor listing](https://app.swapcard.com/widget/event/all-in-2026/exhibitor/RXhoaWJpdG9yXzI1MDQ2MjM=).

**The work includes stage backdrops.** Their homepage lists short films, art books, **stage backdrops**, interactive stories, and collaborative projects with musicians and artists, spanning generative film essays and music video partnerships. Tagline is "Where Images Learn to Dream," and DreamReel is described there as "ATV's Generative AI Studio." The company operates as an imprint by Solutions Aksiom Inc. Source: [abovethevoid.com](https://www.abovethevoid.com/).

Stage backdrops and musician collaborations are the bridge between Colin's weekend gigs and his day job. This is the one studio on the list already making generative visuals for live performance.

**Size and hiring.** Team size beyond the two founders is unverified. No careers or collaboration page exists on the site. Whether they hire engineers is unverified; no careers page was found. Realistically a studio of this size collaborates rather than hires.

**Verdict: seek out, second priority after Mackenzie.** Closest match to CapsLock / Kulfi / Sawhorse in the original list, and the only one with a named, verifiable generative stack. The ask is the meetup and the DreamReel architecture, not a job.

## 4. Laurent Bernier, CCO, Sandscape (Breaking Walls)

**The company behind it.** Sandscape is a product of Breaking Walls, an independent game studio in Montréal, Québec. Co-founders are Laurent Bernier (Creative Director), Sébastien Nadeau (Head of Technology) and Nathanael Dufour, with team experience on AAA titles including Assassin's Creed, Prince of Persia, Far Cry and Rainbow Six. Source: [Breaking Walls on LinkedIn](https://ca.linkedin.com/company/breaking-walls), [breakingwalls.co/about](https://www.breakingwalls.co/about).

Breaking Walls shipped AWAY: The Survival Series, an action-adventure game where the player is a sugar glider, released September 28 2021 on Windows, PS4 and PS5, and June 1 2023 on Xbox. Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Away:_The_Survival_Series), [awayseries.com](https://awayseries.com/).

**Roles on Sandscape.** Sébastien Nadeau is CEO and CTO; Laurent Bernier is Chief Creative Officer and COO. Source: [einpresswire release](https://www.einpresswire.com/article/899909296/sandscape-secures-700-000-to-revolutionize-game-creation-with-ai-and-announces-strategic-partnership-with-crim).

**What Sandscape makes.** Their own site describes it as "a creative studio for making, playing, and sharing browser games," where you "build worlds from words," then refine with visual tools or code, then publish and share. Source: [sandscape.app](https://sandscape.app/) (sandscape.ai 308-redirects here). Press coverage describes it as an AI platform that turns text prompts into playable games, generating environments, mechanics and characters automatically.

**Funding and partners.** $700,000 raised, announced March 2026, with support from Investissement Québec and a strategic partnership with CRIM (Computer Research Institute of Montréal). The CRIM partnership includes dedicated R&D on the generative engine focused on dynamic content synthesis and real-time co-creation. Source: [einpresswire release](https://www.einpresswire.com/article/899909296/sandscape-secures-700-000-to-revolutionize-game-creation-with-ai-and-announces-strategic-partnership-with-crim).

**Engine and tools.** Unverified. Neither the Sandscape site nor the press release names an engine. It is browser-based, which implies a web runtime rather than Unreal or Unity, but that is inference, not a citation.

**Hiring.** No careers page found on sandscape.app or breakingwalls.co. Unverified. At $700k raised, headcount is small. Breaking Walls is at 50 Rue Queen suite 101, Montréal, founded 2015 by three video game industry veterans, and also lists Venus: The Last Ascent and an experimental R&D initiative called **BW Labs** alongside AWAY. Contact info@breakingwalls.co. Source: [breakingwalls.co/about](https://www.breakingwalls.co/about).

**Relevance to Colin.** Real-time generative content, yes. Live visuals for performance, no. This is generative game content, not audiovisual performance. The overlap with Colin's actual pipelines is the orchestration layer: prompt in, structured world out. Genuine but partial.

**Verdict: worth a short conversation, not a priority.** The interesting angle is "real-time co-creation" in the CRIM R&D, and whether a $700k seed studio contracts pipeline engineers. Low probability of a remote senior IC role here given the size and the Montreal games-studio norm of on-site work.

### The market signal, stated plainly

Only three organisations had live, engineer-titled, real-time roles posted at the time of this research: **Dark Slope** (two Unreal virtual production roles), **Wallrus** (Unreal game developer, creative front-end developer) and **Triotech** (software development among roughly 26 openings). Derivative's own community job board carried **zero Canadian postings**.

**Moment Factory, the benchmark, is not hiring for this at all.** Of 18 live postings, none is a TouchDesigner, Unreal or real-time creative role. The technical openings are Full Stack Developer for AI Solutions and Automation, Fullstack Web Developer, Backend Developer, a DevOps/DataOps specialist, an L3 support analyst and five Technical Director roles. Enumerated via [jobs.momentfactory.com/sitemap.xml](https://jobs.momentfactory.com/sitemap.xml), because the listings page renders nothing server-side.

Nor is anyone else. **PixMob** had 12 openings and zero software engineering roles. **Rodeo FX** had 32 openings and not one pipeline, tools, software or R&D role. **Behaviour Interactive** had 38 openings including 10 programming roles, all Montreal-anchored and hybrid.

And on remote: across Moment Factory, PixMob, Rodeo FX and Behaviour, **not one open senior software engineering role is remote, and none publishes a policy that would permit a BC-based remote hire.** Moment Factory is hybrid at 6250 Avenue du Parc with 30 days abroad per year. Behaviour is explicitly in-office Tuesday to Thursday. Rodeo FX requires leadership on site five days a week; its only three remote listings are freelance artist contracts. Several Moment Factory and Rodeo FX postings require French and English bilingualism, which is probably a harder gate than geography.

That is the finding to sit with. The Canadian real-time visuals market is real, concentrated in Montreal and Toronto, almost entirely unposted, and where it is posted it is on-site and often bilingual. It is not a market you enter by applying from Lake Country. It is one you enter by showing work to people who already know each other, which is exactly what a conference plus a MAPP submission plus a TouchIn MTL evening is for.

**And ALL IN may be the wrong room.** ALL IN 2026's five tracks are AI & Society, Applied AI & Enterprise Transformation, AI at the Frontier, AI Data & Infrastructure, and Physical AI & Robotics. There is **no creative industries, entertainment, media or gaming track**; Media & Entertainment is an industry vertical with a single Agora session, not a programme strand. Source: [allinevent.ai/pages/2026-programming](https://allinevent.ai/pages/2026-programming). **E-AI 2027, on February 25-26 2027 at the same Palais des congrès, is a far better-targeted room**, and Moment Factory, Rodeo FX, PixMob and Reimagine AI are all already inside it. See section 22 and the ranking.
